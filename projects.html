PYTORCH · DIFFUSION · MIDI

MIDI Humanization — Diffusion Transformer

When I compose, I usually notate in MuseScore — but exported MIDI often sounds flat. Human pianists shape dynamics, accent, and colour even when the notes on the page stay fixed. This project learns that expressive layer from real performances.

A compact diffusion transformer (about 685k parameters: four layers, RoPE, SwiGLU) is trained on 678 aligned classical recordings from corrected (n)ASAP-style alignments. At inference it rewrites velocity only; pitch and timing stay untouched. Sampling uses a 200-step DDPM denoiser in PyTorch.

Listen & compare on the piano page ↗
Technical notes +

Public demo focuses on the velocity-only checkpoint (~2.6 MB). Examples include an original piece (Perpetuity) and openings of Chopin and Liszt works. Upload your own flat MIDI when the hosted API is connected.

BITEWISE · IOS · PRODUCTION

Bitewise: Production Apps

I built a small studio to bring literature, art, architecture, and scripture off the pedestal and onto a phone. The mission is simple: culture should be democratised — not reserved for people who happened to grow up inside museums and reading rooms.

Literature Bites, Art Bites, Architecture Bites, and Scripture Bites are native SwiftUI apps with on-device Metal rendering. Behind them is a content pipeline that turns curated sources into short-form video, illustrated cards, and 3D monument renders — so each bite feels alive rather than like a slideshow.

The library currently spans 2,323 quotations, 985 artworks, and 641 buildings (and growing). The suite has passed 2,000+ downloads and is still climbing.

QWEN3 · LORA · PYTORCH

Reading List Language Model

This started as an attempt to train a language model just large enough to help me survive an ever-expanding reading list. Training a full model from scratch taught me a lot — but for the voice I wanted, fine-tuning a strong base worked better.

I continued pretraining Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA (rank 16, α 32, dropout 0.05 on all attention and MLP projections) on cleaned text from that list: Russell, Bourdieu, Hofstadter, Wilde, and hundreds of other volumes and essays — about 220 million tokens of curated reading-list material (1,866 documents after cleaning and deduplication). The result is a reading-list transformer you can prompt in the same spirit as the books it was trained on.

An interactive demo is still on the way; the training stack and adapter weights live in my local research repo.

Technical notes +

1024-token chunks, batch 1 with gradient accumulation 16 (16,384 tokens per optimizer step), cosine schedule with 3% warmup, AdamW at 2×10−4. The packed Qwen tokenizer split is ~186M train tokens plus ~4M validation; validation tracks cross-entropy perplexity (not directly comparable to the earlier from-scratch SentencePiece runs).

MODAL LOGIC · PYTORCH

FrameLearner

Classical frame validity in modal logic asks whether there exists a Kripke frame that makes a formula true in every world. FrameLearner reframes that search as a minimax optimisation problem: relations between worlds become differentiable, fuzzy modal operators define a logical loss, and an adversarial valuation hunts for counterexamples while gradient descent updates the frame.

On small eight-world experiments the optimiser sometimes recovers recognisable structure — but low fuzzy loss is not a certificate of classical validity, so the project treats learning as exploratory rather than a proof procedure.
